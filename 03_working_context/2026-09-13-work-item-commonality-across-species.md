# Work Item — What Is Common Across Different Species?

Date: 2026-09-13
Work item ID: WI-2026-09-13-002
Revision ID: WI-2026-09-13-002-r1
Status: IN_PROGRESS

## Objective

Determine which similarities across different biological species are supported by evolutionary evidence, distinguish inherited common features from independently evolved similarities, and identify the strongest explanatory structure for a future content product.

## Requested outcome

A decision-ready research package that answers:

1. What fundamental properties are genuinely shared across cellular life?
2. Which similarities are plausibly inherited from common ancestry?
3. Which similarities evolved independently in unrelated lineages?
4. What does the distinction reveal about constraints and possible solutions in evolution?
5. Which claims are sufficiently supported to become the backbone of a content story, and which remain uncertain or require qualification?

No final publication format is assumed at this stage.

## Scope

Primary scope:
- cellular life and extant species;
- comparative genomics and evolutionary biology;
- shared cellular machinery;
- common ancestry and LUCA;
- convergent evolution;
- selected high-contrast examples.

Out of scope for this pass:
- production of the final article/video/script;
- publication or external distribution;
- broad philosophical claims about the meaning of life;
- treating viruses as species-level evidence for universal cellular properties.

## Initial question

"Что есть общего у разных видов?"

Operationalized as:

"Which similarities across species can be explained by common ancestry, which by recurrent evolutionary constraints or convergence, and how reliably can the two be distinguished?"

## Working hypothesis

The strongest story is unlikely to be a flat inventory of similarities. A more explanatory structure is:

`shared ancestry → inherited biological machinery`

plus

`shared constraints / selection → independently recurring solutions`

with the important boundary that superficial similarity does not by itself establish either common ancestry or functional equivalence.

This is a hypothesis to test, not a promoted conclusion.

## Candidate evidence matrix

| Layer | Candidate question | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| Universal cellular foundations | What mechanisms are shared across extant cellular life? | SUPPORTED by current sources; needs synthesis |
| Common ancestry | Which shared mechanisms are best explained by inheritance? | SUPPORTED in broad terms; exact ancestral reconstruction remains model-dependent |
| Convergence | Can unrelated lineages independently evolve similar complex traits? | STRONGLY SUPPORTED |
| Molecular convergence | Can convergence occur at genomic/protein sequence level? | SUPPORTED, but strength varies by trait/gene |
| Evolutionary constraints | Do physical/ecological constraints channel evolution toward similar solutions? | CANDIDATE EXPLANATORY LAYER; needs targeted evidence |
| Story-level conclusion | How much does this constrain the space of biological solutions? | NOT YET ESTABLISHED |

## Acceptance criteria

- At least three clearly separated mechanisms of similarity are identified.
- At least one strong common-ancestry example is documented.
- At least two independent-convergence examples are documented.
- Each major claim has an evidence class and source.
- Common ancestry and convergence are not conflated.
- Unknowns and limits are explicit.
- A concise story spine can be stated without overstating the evidence.

## Success signals

1. Research produces a decision-changing distinction rather than a list of facts.
2. Evidence can be traced to specific sources.
3. The resulting structure is usable by editorial/production stages without silently converting hypotheses into facts.
4. Final format remains intentionally undecided until the research boundary is complete.
