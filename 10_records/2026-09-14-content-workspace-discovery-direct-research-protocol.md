# Content Workspace — Direct Discovery Research Protocol

Date: 2026-09-14
Status: READY / EXECUTION REQUIRED
Lifecycle stage: Stage 1 — Discovery
Case: Content Factory — End-user Content Workspace / text-source vertical slice

## Research objective

Determine whether a repeated, economically meaningful bottleneck exists between substantive source material and content production, and identify the actual decision/workflow boundary where it occurs.

## Participant hypotheses

Recruit across at least three of these groups:

1. Solo creator / expert with recurring long-form source material.
2. B2B content marketer / content lead.
3. Editor / content strategist.
4. Agency or small content-production team.

Do not create personas before evidence. These are sampling hypotheses only.

## Required sample

Target 6 participants for the first round, with a minimum of 4 and maximum of 8.

Prefer participants who completed at least one substantive source-to-multiple-output workflow within the last 30 days.

## Interview rule

Do not begin by explaining Content Factory, the current hypothesis, or the proposed workspace.

Ask for one recent concrete episode and reconstruct it chronologically. Prefer artifacts over recollection.

## Core protocol

1. Show me the most recent source you turned into more than one content output.
2. What were you trying to achieve with that source?
3. What happened from the moment you received or created the source until the first output was published or delivered?
4. Which tools, files and people were involved?
5. At what point did you decide what was worth turning into content?
6. What ideas, sections or moments did you reject? Why?
7. What did you have to verify or look up outside the source?
8. What did you rewrite manually even after using AI or other automation?
9. Where did another person review or challenge the work?
10. What was repeated, delayed, blocked or abandoned?
11. What was the most expensive part in time, money, attention or missed opportunity?
12. What tool or workaround currently handles that step?
13. What does that workaround fail to solve?
14. If you had to remove one step from the workflow without reducing quality, which step would you remove?
15. What makes you decide that an idea is worth producing rather than leaving it unused?

## Artifact collection

Where participants can safely share them, collect or inspect:

- original source;
- transcript or notes;
- brief or editorial plan;
- extracted ideas/moments;
- prompts;
- drafts;
- review comments;
- revision history;
- final outputs;
- publication/distribution record.

Do not request confidential customer, employer or personal data unnecessarily.

## Coding dimensions

For each episode record:

- source_type
- intended_outcome
- audience_definition
- idea_extraction_method
- story_or_angle_selection_method
- evidence_verification_method
- ai_usage
- human_editorial_decisions
- production_steps
- review_steps
- distribution_steps
- waiting_or_blocking_points
- rework_points
- abandonment_points
- time_cost
- people_cost
- financial_cost
- quality_cost
- current_workaround
- unresolved_problem
- frequency

## Falsification tests

The editorial-intelligence hypothesis weakens if most participants:

- already identify content opportunities quickly;
- describe execution/distribution as the dominant cost;
- use existing tools with little unresolved friction;
- cannot identify meaningful rejected ideas or editorial decisions;
- do not need source/evidence traceability;
- experience the problem too rarely to justify a dedicated workflow.

It strengthens if multiple independent episodes show:

- repeated source-to-opportunity selection work;
- significant judgment or time before production;
- repeated rejection/selection decisions;
- weak support from current tools;
- meaningful review/rework caused by poor upstream selection or unsupported claims;
- a user segment with sufficient frequency and economic consequence.

## Output

After 4–8 interviews, produce a cross-case evidence matrix and classify each hypothesis as:

`SUPPORTED / PLAUSIBLE / UNCONFIRMED / WEAKENED / REJECTED`

Only after that synthesis may Stage 1 produce a candidate Problem/Opportunity statement. No Product Decision follows automatically; it requires its own gate.
