## P05 — Bangkok street life (Paul Iddon, 2008)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bangkok_street_life.jpg
Observed: dense roadside scene with motorcycles and helmets in foreground; person reading a newspaper near center, pedestrian in light clothing on right; umbrellas and signage obscure portions of buildings and people. People occupy different depths and do not all face the camera. The frame contains competing objects rather than an isolated hero subject. Umbrella-covered areas are visibly darker than open street, but the exact source-light geometry cannot be inferred from the single image.
Verified EXIF: Nikon D200; 28 mm physical focal length (42 mm full-frame equivalent as recorded); f/5.6; 1/125 s; ISO 400; no flash; cloudy light-source metadata. Do not confuse 28 mm physical with full-frame equivalent.
Transferable heuristic: ordinary street photos can keep clutter, partial occlusions, non-central subjects and context-specific activity; do not force every subject into portrait isolation.
Limits: single image does not establish what editing occurred, nor camera-to-subject distance.

## P09 — Street Photography Portrait (Flavio~, 2010)
Source: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Street_Photography_Portrait_(4334741366).jpg
Observed: monochrome close-up of a woman looking down and to camera-left; head dominates right side; bright, partly blown-out foliage and sky at left; asymmetric lighting across face; background does not compete with face. Soft-to-sharp transitions and distinct highlights remain, not uniformly perfect exposure. This is a *portrait*, not evidence that all real photos are candid.
Verified EXIF: Canon PowerShot SX1 IS; 21.016 mm physical focal length; f/4.5; 1/20 s; ISO 200; flash off; manual white balance. Do not claim lens-equivalent focal length or exact source direction without calibration.
Transferable heuristic: a convincing portrait may include strong local highlight differences and bright background areas without artificially balancing every region.
Limits: black-and-white conversion prevents conclusions about original scene colors.
